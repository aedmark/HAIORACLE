Phrase Normalization & Deterministic Seed Hashing

#
foundationsdeterminismsecurity

Transforms arbitrary human text input into an immutable unsigned 32-bit seed.

What it does

Accepts user text up to 72 characters, trims surrounding whitespace, collapses internal whitespace sequences into single ASCII spaces, converts text to lowercase, and hashes the normalized string into an unsigned 32-bit integer. Rejects empty phrases or whitespace-only inputs at the boundary.

How it works

The normalization and hashing pipeline follows a strict pure functional path in src/score.mjs:

  1. normalizePhrase() applies Unicode normalization, lowercasing, and regex space collapsing s.trim().toLowerCase().replace(/\s+/g, ' ').
  2. hashPhrase() executes a 32-bit FNV-1a non-cryptographic dispersion algorithm using offset basis 0x811c9dc5 and prime 0x01000193.
  3. Bitwise operations (Math.imul and >>> 0) guarantee that the resulting integer remains unsigned and bit-identical across JavaScript engines on all platforms.

Why it works this way

A creature's identity depends on exact determinism: confiding identical words must invariably summon the identical specimen. Using FNV-1a on normalized text avoids cross-browser string hashing discrepancies while forgiving accidental double spaces and letter case differences without altering semantics (ADR D-003).

What it does

Overcomes the ensemble's continuous wall of sound by executing a purpose-built 1972 INTERCAL interpreter across the 12-step loop. How it works

src/intercal.mjs and src/conductor.i execute genuine INTERCAL 1972 rules:

  1. Statement Politeness Checking: enforces the INTERCAL 1972 politeness rule (0.20 <= P/N <= 0.334), rejecting impolite programs with ICL079 and groveling programs with ICL099. The conductor score executes with 8 polite statements out of 35 (22.9%).
  2. Per-phrase form: the host avalanche-mixes the score seed into a 16-bit arrangement word, and DO WRITE IN .5 / .6 read it and its complement. Seven select (~) choices then decide who colours the opening, who answers the choir, what sits under the breakdown, and whether the turnaround (#3 $ .7, a mingle) starts on step 10 or 11. Guardrails keep at least five band voices on every step and pulse and bass under the first three sections (D-032).
  3. Registers are the arrangement: twospot registers :1..:8 hold one channel each (#1 plays, #0 rests), .1 <- #n opens section n (Exposition, Antiphony, Tutti, Breakdown), and the VM snapshots the registers at each marker, so the program itself decides who plays. Malformed form is rejected at summon.
  4. PLEASE IGNORE :8 freezes the Fun Machine register, so the breakdown's DO :8 <- #0 does nothing and the player is never rested.
  5. COME FROM (60) Turnaround Cadence: .3 starts the turnaround on step 10 or 11 and COME FROM skips the lead's exit, so pulse, bass, and lead come home through the loop's end.
  6. IntercalVM bitwise operations: supports authentic 16-bit and 32-bit registers, bitwise mingle ($) and select (~), statement labels, and GIVE UP termination.
  7. The workstation shows the form: the transport names the section, console strips light TACET while rested, MIDI exports carry section Marker events, and the stem README lists section times.

Why it works this way

In real ensemble performance, musicality requires breathing space, call-and-response dynamics, and sectional contrast rather than an unceasing wall of sound where every instrument blasts at maximum density on every step (ADR D-028).
